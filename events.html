The Six Events

Floor Exercise

The floor routine uses the entire 40'x40' mat and is made up mostly of tumbling passes in different directions, with required acrobatic elements and at least one non-acrobatic element usually preformed in a corner or edge of the floor, all within 70 seconds. Gymnasts link difficult passes with multiple flips and twists to increase there difficulty and score

Pommel Horse

Considered one of the hardest mens events, pommel horse routines are continuous circular movements broken up only by the required scissors elements, with only the hands touching the apparatus. Gymnasts spend most of the routine on one arm and can never stop or pause, so mistakes have to be corrected while still moving.

Still Rings

Rings routines require stillness, a straight body, and control. They include a swing to handstand and at least two strength elements (such as a iron cross or Maltese) held for two seconds. The event demands lots of upper body strength, and among top gymnasts the winner often comes down to little mistakes

Vault

A good vault is quick, explosive, and big, with height, distance, speed, and a stuck landing. Each vault has its own value based on how complex it is, and the gymnast must stay focused since the whole performance happens in seconds.

Parallel Bars

Parallel bars routines consist mostly of swing and flight elements, with no more than three stops or holds and a required under swing. The hardest skills can make the gymnast briefly lose sight of the bars, which calls for strong hand eye coordination and timing.

High Bar

High bar combines continuous swings, release moves, and high flying dismounts. Release moves happen 12 to 15 feet above the bar, so winning requires several releases with clean form, and a stuck dismount.
